Grammar lab · Apply the rules

1. Describe pain
عِنْدِي أَلَمٌ فِي الْحَلْقِ.

Use عِنْدِي أَلَمٌ فِي + body part. Keep statements simple and accurate.

2. Duration
مُنْذُ يَوْمَيْنِ.

Use مُنْذُ + time expression to say since when/for how long in simple speech.

3. Feminine agreement
الصَّيْدَلِيَّةُ مَفْتُوحَةٌ.

Pharmacy is feminine, so the adjective is feminine.

4. Question pattern
مُنْذُ مَتَى؟ / هَلْ عِنْدَكَ حُمَّى؟

مُنْذُ مَتَى asks 'since when?'; هَلْ asks yes/no. A clinician decides medical care; this is only language practice.

Quick teacher drills

Drill 1: Ask which body part hurts, then how long.
Drill 2: Practice asking for a pharmacy and opening time.
Drill 3: Role-play a clinician asking basic questions; avoid suggesting diagnoses or doses.

Independent production & lesson review

Task 1 (no notes): Student creates four personal statements and four questions related to this week's topic.

Task 2 (surprise): Teacher changes one piece of information each turn (person, location, time, or reason).

Task 3 (recall): Retrieve five expressions from earlier lessons and weave two into the current role-play.

Feedback: The teacher writes three corrections in simple English. The student records the same 60-second task again, applying corrections.

Homework: 5 min aloud after class, 5 min next day, 5 min after 3 days. This is a practice routine, not a guarantee of retention.
