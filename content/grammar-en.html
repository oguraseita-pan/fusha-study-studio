LESSON 01 · A0 · ENGLISH EDITION

The Arabic alphabet and connected letters

Arabic is written from right to left. Start by recognizing each letter and its sounds, then practise how letters join.

Core rules

1
Arabic has 28 main letters. Most change shape at the start, middle and end of a word.
2
Six letters do not join to the next letter on their left: ا د ذ ر ز و.
3
Dots matter: ب, ت and ث are different letters.
4
Ordinary texts often omit short-vowel marks. Learn with vowelled texts first.

Read, listen and repeat

بَاب
bāb
Door
بَيْت
bayt
House
وَلَد
walad
Boy
ثَوْب
thawb
Thobe; garment
Speaking and teaching note

Read accurately before trying to read quickly; focus on dots and vowels.

Self-check

  1. How do ب and ت differ?
  2. Name three letters that do not connect to the next letter.
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. The dots: ب has one below, ت two above.
  2. For example: د, ر, و.
LESSON 02 · A0 · ENGLISH EDITION

Short vowels, long vowels and sukūn

Arabic contrasts short a/i/u with long ā/ī/ū. Length can change the meaning of a word.

Core rules

1
Fatḥa َ gives a, kasra ِ gives i, and ḍamma ُ gives u.
2
Long ā, ī and ū are generally written with ا, ي and و, in the appropriate pattern.
3
Sukūn ْ means that the consonant has no following short vowel.
4
Practise vowel length with a native speaker, not just romanization.

Read, listen and repeat

كَتَبَ
kataba
He wrote
كِتَاب
kitāb
Book
كَبِير
kabīr
Big (masculine)
نُور
nūr
Light
Speaking and teaching note

Spoken MSA often drops word-final case vowels; vowels inside the word still matter.

日本語メモ： 語末の格語尾は会話で省かれることが多くても、単語内部の長短母音は区別します。

Self-check

  1. What is the difference between kataba and kitāb?
  2. Name the usual letters for the three long vowels.
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. Kataba is a verb (he wrote); kitāb is a noun (book) with long ā.
  2. Alif, yāʾ and wāw in suitable patterns.
LESSON 03 · A0 · ENGLISH EDITION

Shadda, doubled consonants and sun letters

Shadda marks a doubled or lengthened consonant; it is not a stress mark.

Core rules

1
A letter with shadda, such as رّ or مّ, is pronounced with a longer consonant: rr, mm.
2
Doubling consonants may create a different word or verb pattern.
3
With the definite article ال, the l assimilates to a following sun letter.
4
Sun letters: ت ث د ذ ر ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ل ن; other letters are moon letters.

Read, listen and repeat

دَرَسَ
darasa
He studied
دَرَّسَ
darrasa
He taught
الشَّمْس
ash-shams
The sun
الْقَمَر
al-qamar
The moon
Speaking and teaching note

Double consonants are important in both MSA and spoken dialects; yalla has a long l.

Self-check

  1. Does shadda lengthen a vowel or a consonant?
  2. Why do we say ash-shams rather than al-shams?
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. A consonant.
  2. Because ش is a sun letter and assimilates the l of ال.
LESSON 04 · A0 · ENGLISH EDITION

Hamza, alif, alif maqṣūra and tāʾ marbūṭa

Similar-looking letters must be distinguished by sound, spelling and grammatical function.

Core rules

1
Hamza ء represents a glottal stop. Alif ا often supports hamza or marks long ā.
2
At the beginning, hamzat al-qaṭʿ is normally أ for a/u and إ for i. Hamzat al-waṣl often appears as plain ا.
3
In the middle, the vowels of hamza and the preceding letter usually compete: kasra > ḍamma > fatḥa. Long-vowel environments have exceptions.
4
At the end, the preceding vowel usually decides the seat: i → ئ, u → ؤ, a → أ, no short vowel → ء.
5
Final ى represents long ā; final ة marks tāʾ marbūṭa, often feminine.

Read, listen and repeat

أُمّ
ʾumm
Mother
اِسْم
ism
Name
سُئِلَ
suʾila
He was asked
قَارِئ
qāriʾ
Reader
Speaking and teaching note

In real reading, do not treat ا/ى or ت/ة as interchangeable, even when an app groups them pedagogically.

日本語メモ： ハムザの「台」は語頭・語中・語末で決め方が異なります。非接続文字が隣にあるだけでは基本規則は変わりません。

Self-check

  1. Which position does initial hamza take before i?
  2. Why does qāriʾ end with ئ?
  3. Are ت and ة identical spellings?
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. Below the alif: إ.
  2. The preceding vowel is kasra (i).
  3. No; they have distinct spelling and grammatical functions.
LESSON 05 · A0 · ENGLISH EDITION

Masculine and feminine nouns

Arabic nouns have grammatical gender, even when they refer to objects rather than people.

Core rules

1
Many feminine nouns and adjectives end in ة, but not all.
2
Gender affects adjectives, demonstratives and verb agreement.
3
Learn gender together with each new noun.

Read, listen and repeat

طَالِب
ṭālib
Male student
طَالِبَة
ṭāliba
Female student
كَبِير
kabīr
Big (masculine)
كَبِيرَة
kabīra
Big (feminine)
Speaking and teaching note

Spoken Arabic still distinguishes masculine and feminine agreement, even when case endings are dropped.

Self-check

  1. Give the feminine form of ṭālib.
  2. Must every feminine noun end in ة?
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. ṭāliba / طالبة.
  2. No; there are exceptions.
LESSON 06 · A0 · ENGLISH EDITION

Definite and indefinite nouns

Arabic marks definiteness mainly with the article ال, unlike English a/the.

Core rules

1
An indefinite noun generally has no article; full MSA often shows tanwīn when the noun is declinable.
2
The definite article ال corresponds roughly to the English the.
3
The article is written even when its l is assimilated before a sun letter.
4
The article and tanwīn do not normally appear together.

Read, listen and repeat

كِتَاب
kitāb
A book (indefinite)
الْكِتَاب
al-kitāb
The book (definite)
بَيْت
bayt
A house (indefinite)
الْبَيْت
al-bayt
The house (definite)
Speaking and teaching note

Word-final case endings are often not pronounced in ordinary conversation, but the article remains useful.

Self-check

  1. How do you turn kitāb into the book?
  2. Can a noun normally have both ال and tanwīn?
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. Add the article: al-kitāb.
  2. No, the normal definite article excludes tanwīn.

LESSON 07 · A1 · ENGLISH EDITION

Personal pronouns and demonstratives

Pronouns show who is speaking, being addressed or discussed. Some forms differ by gender and number.

Core rules

1
أنا means I; نحن means we.
2
أنتَ and أنتِ distinguish a male and female addressee.
3
هو / هي mean he / she.
4
هذا and هذه mean this for masculine and feminine nouns.

Read, listen and repeat

أَنَا
anā
I
أَنْتَ / أَنْتِ
anta / anti
You (male) / you (female)
هٰذَا كِتَاب
hādhā kitāb
This is a book.
هٰذِهِ سَيَّارَة
hādhihi sayyāra
This is a car.
Speaking and teaching note

Practise both asking someone a question and answering it; real conversation requires both.

Self-check

  1. How do you say you to one woman?
  2. Choose the demonstrative for a feminine noun.
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. أنتِ (anti).
  2. هذه (hādhihi).
LESSON 08 · A1 · ENGLISH EDITION

Nominal sentences: A is B

A present-time sentence with a noun or adjective predicate normally has no written verb for is/are.

Core rules

1
A basic nominal sentence is subject + predicate, for example: I am an employee.
2
The subject is often definite; the predicate may be indefinite.
3
Negation commonly uses forms of ليس.
4
The predicate usually appears in the nominative in formal, fully vowelled MSA.

Read, listen and repeat

أَنَا مُوَظَّفٌ
anā muwaẓẓaf(un)
I am an employee.
الْمَكْتَبُ قَرِيبٌ
al-maktabu qarīb(un)
The office is nearby.
أَنَا يَابَانِيّ
anā yābānī
I am Japanese (male speaker).
هُوَ مُدَرِّس
huwa mudarris
He is a teacher.
الْجَوُّ حَارّ
al-jawwu ḥārr
The weather is hot.
لَسْتُ مُدَرِّسًا
lastu mudarrisan
I am not a teacher.
Speaking and teaching note

In conversation, say short complete sentences before worrying about every final case vowel.

日本語メモ： 名詞文の現在肯定文には英語のbe動詞に相当する語を通常置きません。否定ではلَيْسَを使います。

Self-check

  1. How can you say I am a teacher without using a present-tense word for am?
  2. What expression negates a nominal sentence?
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. Use a subject and an indefinite nominal predicate: أنا مدرس.
  2. ليس and its conjugated forms.
LESSON 09 · A1 · ENGLISH EDITION

Adjectives and agreement

An adjective normally follows the noun it describes and agrees with it.

Core rules

1
The adjective typically agrees with the noun in gender, number, definiteness and case.
2
A definite noun followed by a definite adjective usually forms one noun phrase: the big house.
3
A definite noun followed by an indefinite predicate adjective usually forms a sentence: the house is big.
4
Non-human plural nouns commonly take feminine singular adjective agreement.

Read, listen and repeat

بَيْتٌ كَبِيرٌ
baytun kabīrun
A big house (indefinite)
الْبَيْتُ الْكَبِيرُ
al-baytu l-kabīru
The big house (definite)
سَيَّارَةٌ جَدِيدَةٌ
sayyāratun jadīdatun
A new car (feminine)
بَيْتٌ كَبِيرٌ
baytun kabīrun
A big house
الْبَيْتُ الْكَبِيرُ
al-baytu l-kabīru
The big house
الْبَيْتُ كَبِيرٌ
al-baytu kabīrun
The house is big.
سَيَّارَةٌ جَدِيدَةٌ
sayyāratun jadīdatun
A new car
Speaking and teaching note

Gender and number agreement matter in conversation; full case endings are less urgent for spoken fluency.

日本語メモ： 「大きな家」と「家は大きい」は定冠詞の付き方が違います。名詞＋定の形容詞は名詞句、名詞＋不定の形容詞は文になる場合があります。

Self-check

  1. Compare the big house with the house is big.
  2. Why is the adjective feminine in a phrase with sayyāra?
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. The big house has two definite elements: البيت الكبير; the house is big has an indefinite adjective predicate: البيت كبير.
  2. Because sayyāra is grammatically feminine.
LESSON 10 · A1 · ENGLISH EDITION

Prepositions of place and direction

Prepositions help describe location, destination, origin and company.

Core rules

1
في means in/at; إلى means to; من means from; مع means with.
2
A noun following a true preposition is generally genitive in fully vowelled MSA.
3
Memorize the noun and its preposition together in useful phrases.

Read, listen and repeat

فِي دُبَي
fī Dubayy
In Dubai
أَنَا فِي الْمَكْتَب
anā fī l-maktab
I am in the office.
إِلَى الْمَطَار
ilā l-maṭār
To the airport
مَعَ صَدِيقِي
maʿa ṣadīqī
With my friend
Speaking and teaching note

These short phrases are very practical for describing your workplace, transport and meetings.

Self-check

  1. Which preposition means to a destination?
  2. What happens to the case of a noun after في?
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. إلى (ilā).
  2. Genitive.
LESSON 11 · A1 · ENGLISH EDITION

Possession with iḍāfa

The iḍāfa construction links nouns, usually indicating ownership, association or type.

Core rules

1
The first noun is the thing or head noun; the second identifies its owner or association.
2
The second noun must be genitive.
3
The first noun normally does not take the definite article or tanwīn within a regular iḍāfa.
4
To say have, everyday MSA often uses عند plus a possessive ending or لي.

Read, listen and repeat

اِسْمُ الشَّرِكَةِ
ismu sh-sharikati
The company's name
عِنْدِي سُؤَالٌ
ʿindī suʾālun
I have a question.
عِنْدَهُ سَيَّارَةٌ
ʿindahu sayyāratun
He has a car.
كِتَابُ الطَّالِبِ
kitābu ṭ-ṭālibi
The student's book
مَكْتَبُ الشَّرِكَةِ
maktabu sh-sharikati
The company's office
عِنْدِي سَيَّارَة
ʿindī sayyāra
I have a car.
لِي سُؤَال
lī suʾāl
I have a question.
Speaking and teaching note

Make practical expressions such as customer name, company office and sales report.

日本語メモ： イダーファでは2語目が属格、通常1語目にはالやタンウィーンを付けません。1語目の格は文の役割で決まります。

Self-check

  1. What case does the second noun take?
  2. How do you distinguish the company's office from I have an office?
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. Genitive.
  2. maktabu sh-sharikati versus ʿindī maktabun, for example.
LESSON 12 · A1 · ENGLISH EDITION

Possessive suffixes

Arabic often attaches my/your/his/her to the end of a noun rather than using a separate word.

Core rules

1
ـي can mean my; ـكَ means your (to a man); ـكِ your (to a woman).
2
ـهُ and ـهَا can mean his and her.
3
Some nouns change shape before a suffix, particularly ة → ت.
4
The same family of suffixes also occurs with verbs and prepositions.

Read, listen and repeat

كِتَابِي
kitābī
My book
كِتَابُكَ
kitābuka
Your book (to a man)
كِتَابُهَا
kitābuhā
Her book
سَيَّارَتِي
sayyāratī
My car
Speaking and teaching note

Use these suffixes in introductions, addresses, possessions and business-related questions.

Self-check

  1. How do you say my book?
  2. What suffix would you use for her book?
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. كتابي (kitābī).
  2. ـها (hā).
LESSON 13 · A1 · ENGLISH EDITION

Yes/no questions and question words

Form questions with common particles and interrogatives.

Core rules

1
هل introduces many yes/no questions.
2
أين asks where; متى when; كيف how; ماذا/ما what; لماذا why; كم how many/how much.
3
A simple question often keeps its basic sentence order.
4
Learn to switch between asking and answering the same question.

Read, listen and repeat

هَلْ تَعْمَلُ هُنَا؟
hal taʿmalu hunā?
Do you work here? (to a man)
أَيْنَ تَعْمَلُ؟
ayna taʿmalu?
Where do you work? (to a man)
مَتَى يَبْدَأُ الْاِجْتِمَاعُ؟
matā yabdaʾu l-ijtimāʿu?
When does the meeting start?
أَيْنَ الْمَكْتَب؟
ayna l-maktab?
Where is the office?
مَا هٰذَا؟
mā hādhā?
What is this?
كَيْفَ حَالُكَ؟
kayfa ḥāluka?
How are you? (to a man)
هَلْ عِنْدَكَ وَقْت؟
hal ʿindaka waqt?
Do you have time? (to a man)
Speaking and teaching note

In practice, use one clear question at a time and ask for repetition when needed.

Self-check

  1. How do you ask Where do you work?
  2. What is the function of هل?
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. أين تعمل؟
  2. It introduces a yes/no question.
LESSON 14 · A1 · ENGLISH EDITION

Perfect verbs (past tense)

The perfect form commonly describes completed or past actions. Subject information is often marked by suffixes.

Core rules

1
The dictionary citation form is often third-person masculine singular: كَتَبَ = he wrote.
2
Suffix ـتُ means I did; ـتَ is you (male); ـتِ is you (female).
3
The third-person feminine singular often ends in ـتْ.
4
Many Arabic sentences begin with a verb, so pay attention to the suffix.

Read, listen and repeat

كَتَبْتُ رِسَالَةً
katabtu risālatan
I wrote a letter.
ذَهَبْتُ إِلَى الْمَكْتَب
dhahabtu ilā l-maktab
I went to the office.
وَصَلَ الْعَمِيلُ
waṣala l-ʿamīlu
The customer arrived.
وَصَلَتْ فَاطِمَةُ
waṣalat Fāṭimatu
Fatima arrived.
Speaking and teaching note

Narrate yesterday's activities in simple past-tense sentences.

日本語メモ： 完了形は英語の過去形に近い場面で頻用しますが、英語の時制と完全に1対1ではありません。

Self-check

  1. What does ـتُ mean in katabtu?
  2. How do you express She arrived using the perfect form?
Suggested answers (show / hide)
  1. First-person singular, I.
  2. وصلت فاطمة (waṣalat Fāṭimatu).
LESSON 15 · A1 · ENGLISH EDITION

Imperfect verbs (present and habitual)

The imperfect expresses present, habitual and sometimes future actions. It uses prefixes and sometimes suffixes.

Core rules

1
The four main person prefixes are أ, ن, ت, ي.
2
For kataba / yaktubu: أكتب I write, نكتب we write, تكتب you (male) or she writes, يكتب he writes.
3
You must also check suffixes: you (female) write is تكتبين.
4
Imperfect stem vowels vary by verb; memorize the perfect and imperfect together.

Read, listen and repeat

أَنَا أَكْتُبُ رِسَالَةً
anā aktubu risālatan
I am writing a message.
هِيَ تَكْتُبُ رِسَالَةً
hiya taktubu risālatan
She is writing a message.
أَعْمَلُ فِي دُبَي
aʿmalu fī Dubayy
I work in Dubai.
أَكْتُبُ رِسَالَةً
aktubu risālatan
I am writing a letter.

SCOPE AND FUTURE STUDY

Does this book fully cover CEFR B2 grammar?

Short answer: No. It introduces the main grammatical structures from beginner to upper-intermediate study, but B1–B2 chapters are deliberately compact. It is a teaching map and a foundation, not a complete reference grammar, not a course with hours of authentic input and not proof of B2 ability.

Areas for a future advanced volume

  • Numbers 11–99, ordinal numbers, dates and the full rules of counted nouns.
  • Systematic weak-verb and hamzated-verb paradigms, including hollow, assimilated and final-weak verbs across moods and derived forms.
  • Full conjugation and meaning ranges of derived verb Forms II–X, their maṣdars and participles.
  • Diptotes, irregular declension and deeper syntax: ḥāl, tamyīz, absolute object (mafʿūl muṭlaq), exception and vocative constructions.
  • Complex conditionals, negation, clause linking, relative clauses, government and register-specific usage.
  • Authentic B1–B2 listening, extended reading, discussion, writing correction and assessment tasks.
日本語の補足： B2までの文法を「概観する」ためには利用できますが、「B2文法を網羅した辞典」ではありません。先生には各課の正確さ・自然さを確認してもらい、実際に発話で使えるようにしてください。

Recommended focus for the next five months

Prioritise A0–A1 accuracy, early A2 structures and repeated practical MSA conversation. Review a small number of target forms in three lessons each week. Use native-speaker corrections, recorded dialogues and new scenarios rather than rushing into abstract B2 material.
